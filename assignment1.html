Scenario

The Multiplayer Synthesizer scenario challenges the conventional assumption that digital musical instruments are primarily designed for individual users.
I interpret this scenario as an opportunity to explore how a browser-based instrument can make musical creation a shared and collaborative experience between two or more people using the same computer.
Rather than requiring users to have prior musical knowledge or experience with synthesizers, the instrument should provide a low-barrier way for participants to experiment, respond to one another and contribute to a shared sound.
Its purpose is to allow users to create something new and enjoyable through collaboration, where musical expression emerges from the interaction between participants rather than from individual technical skill.
The worth of the project lies in creating an inclusive, beginner-friendly and socially engaging creative experience, where people with different levels of musical experience can participate meaningfully and share creative agency.

Responses

The proposed project is a cooperative browser-based synthesizer designed for two or more users to create music together. The instrument will distribute complementary forms of control between participants, encouraging users to listen, respond and experiment with each other's input rather than simply operating independent instruments.
Collaboration will therefore become an essential part of the musical interaction, with the shared sound emerging from the combined actions of the participants.
The interface will avoid the complexity of conventional synthesizers by presenting a limited set of expressive and understandable controls that can be explored without prior musical knowledge.
This approach responds to the scenario's purpose by transforming music-making into a shared, playful and exploratory activity, while its value is established through inclusive participation, low barriers to entry and social engagement.
The project aims to demonstrate that meaningful musical expression can emerge from cooperation rather than technical expertise.

Framing

The project will be framed around three interconnected principles: simplified interaction, shared control and collaborative creative agency.
The interface will use a simplified UI to reduce the cognitive and technical barriers commonly associated with synthesizers, focusing on a small number of expressive controls rather than reproducing the complexity of professional music production software.
Controls will be distributed between participants so that each user can influence a complementary aspect of the sound, while the resulting audio remains part of a shared musical system. A split-screen layout, divided control areas or multiple browser windows may be explored as possible interface structures for supporting simultaneous participation; the final spatial arrangement will be informed by interaction design research and testing rather than being predetermined.
The project will focus on local, simultaneous and playful interaction, with experimentation and communication between participants forming part of the instrument's characterisation. It will exclude complex music-production workflows, advanced synthesis parameters, individual user accounts and remote online multiplayer functionality.
This framing positions the project as a collaborative creative instrument rather than a conventional synthesizer, with the interface designed to encourage experimentation, shared ownership and accessible participation.

Interaction Design Analysis

UN / CYCLE - Auren Snyder

UN / Cycle demonstrates how an extremely minimal interface can turn a simple physical gesture into a musical interaction.
The interface is divided vertically into two areas by a central boundary, and users can draw across the screen with their mouse.
When the gesture crosses the boundary, it generates sound, making the relationship between movement and audio immediately understandable through direct interaction.
This is particularly effective for the proposed project because it does not require users to understand musical notation, scales or conventional synthesiser controls before they can participate.
The simplicity of the interaction creates a playful and exploratory character, supporting the project's purpose of allowing people without musical experience to create something new and fun.
The divided screen also provides a useful reference for the cooperative synthesizer, as separate spatial areas could represent the contributions of different participants.
However, the interface is intentionally limited in the amount of control it provides over the resulting sound. There are few opportunities for users to meaningfully modify or develop the audio beyond the core interaction.
Therefore, while the project will take inspiration from its spatial division and low-barrier interaction, it will avoid reducing the instrument to a single gesture.
My proposal should retain this simplicity while providing additional expressive controls that allow participants to influence different aspects of a shared sound.

OTS-01 - bellicapelli

OTS-01 provides an accessible model for interacting with a digital keyboard through both physical and on-screen input.
Users can trigger sounds by pressing the corresponding keys on their computer keyboard or by directly interacting with the visual keyboard using a mouse.
This dual-input approach is valuable for the proposed cooperative synthesizer because it allows the instrument to accommodate different interaction preferences without requiring specialised musical hardware.
The visual design of the keyboard is also particularly relevant to the project's aim of reducing barriers for beginners.
Rather than relying heavily on conventional musical notation or complex technical terminology, its interface communicates musical interaction through recognisable visual elements.
This supports the scenario's purpose of enabling users without musical experience to participate immediately and supports the project's worth of inclusive and beginner-friendly interaction.
The project will therefore explore both keyboard and pointer-based interaction, allowing participants to choose or combine different input methods.
However, the proposed instrument will extend this principle beyond reproducing a traditional keyboard by using visual controls and metaphors to communicate sound parameters without requiring users to understand music theory.
This will help ensure that the interface communicates what users can do through interaction itself rather than relying on specialist musical knowledge.
