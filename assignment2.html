Mapping Design Prototypes

My first area of exploration focuses on the mapping and layout of the cooperative synthesizer. The instrument is designed for two people to interact with the same sound, with Player 01 controlling spatial input, waveform and volume, while Player 02 controls the piano-style keyboard.

I am exploring how the interface can communicate these two complementary roles through the spatial arrangement of the controls. The prototypes test different ways of dividing the screen while keeping the main interaction and visual language consistent. The main areas of interest are clarity, approachability and how easily two people can understand where their own interaction space begins and how it connects to the other player's controls.

Prototype 1: Top / Bottom Layout

This prototype is the baseline layout from Draft 03. The interface is divided vertically between the two participants. Player 01 uses the upper area for spatial input, waveform and volume controls, while Player 02 uses the lower area as a piano-style keyboard.

This prototype explores whether a simple top-and-bottom division can make each player's role easy to understand while keeping both interaction spaces visible at the same time.

Prototype 2: Left / Right Layout

This prototype changes the player distribution from a vertical division to a horizontal division. Player 02's keyboard is placed on the left, while Player 01's spatial and sound controls are placed on the right.

The main interaction remains consistent with the baseline so that the spatial relationship between the two players becomes the main area of exploration. This prototype explores whether a side-by-side arrangement changes how naturally two people can interact with the synthesizer together.

Prototype 3: Central Spatial Layout

This prototype places the spatial input closer to the centre of the interface while keeping the keyboard as a distinct area for Player 02. The waveform and volume controls remain associated with Player 01, but the spatial interaction becomes a stronger visual focus within the shared interface.

This explores whether positioning the spatial interaction closer to the centre can make the synthesizer feel more like one shared instrument rather than two separate control areas.

Spatial Input Design Prototypes

My second area of exploration focuses on spatial input and how physical movement can influence the shared sound. Rather than using traditional synthesizer controls alone, these prototypes explore how position, movement and gesture can provide a more approachable way for users to experiment with sound.

The spatial interaction is designed as Player 01's main way of shaping the sound, while Player 02 selects notes using the piano-style keyboard. I am interested in how different spatial interactions can communicate the relationship between movement and sound, and whether they can make the collaborative relationship between the two players more expressive and understandable.

Prototype 1: Pitch + Brightness

This prototype is the baseline spatial interaction from Draft 03. Player 01 drags a ball within a two-dimensional sound space. Horizontal movement changes brightness, while vertical movement changes pitch. The top-right represents a higher and brighter sound, while the bottom-left represents a lower and darker sound.

This prototype explores a direct relationship between physical position and sound, allowing users to discover the mapping through movement rather than requiring them to understand technical synthesizer controls.

Prototype 2: Sound Modifier

This prototype changes the role of spatial input. Instead of controlling the pitch, Player 02's keyboard determines the note while Player 01 uses the spatial area to modify how that note sounds. Horizontal movement changes brightness, while vertical movement changes the strength of the sound effect.

This explores a more complementary relationship between the two players: one player chooses the musical notes while the other shapes their sound. The prototype tests whether separating these responsibilities can make the collaborative relationship clearer.

Prototype 3: Spatial Gesture

This prototype changes both the form and behaviour of the spatial interaction. Instead of dragging a ball to a fixed position, Player 01 draws a gesture directly within the spatial area. The direction and movement of the gesture influence the sound, while movement speed affects the intensity of the modulation.

This prototype explores whether a gesture-based interaction can make spatial input feel more expressive and physical. It also tests whether continuous movement and gesture paths can create a stronger sense of collaboration with the player controlling the keyboard.
